Does recursion have anything left to infer?

Phases 1 and 2 tested a recursive trunk under the one delay condition where the theory says recursion is unnecessary — and got the result that condition predicts. Phase 3 removes the three confounds, tests recursion where the conditioning genuinely leaves something to infer, and opens the axis the project has never measured: whether any of these trunks makes a usable controller.

01

The null was pre-ordained

Not wrong — pre-ordained. Every number Phase 1 and Phase 2 produced stands. What they cannot do is answer the question they were built to ask.

Under FixedDelay(1) with augment_obs=true, the queue of pending actions appended to the observation is exact. Our own augmentation.md §6 verifies it against a recorded action sequence — five delay values × ten steps, zero mismatches — and Katsikopoulos & Engelbrecht (2003) prove the augmented state is a sufficient statistic for the delayed MDP. An architecture whose hypothesised advantage is inferring missing state was handed a task with no missing state, and charged 5–14× the wall-clock for the privilege.

That is not a fringe reading of the design. It is written into the repository:

For StochasticDelay/TimeVaryingDelay, whose per-step d can be less than max_delay … the exposed “pending queue” is a reasonable proxy … rather than a proven sufficient statistic. Extending §3's guarantee to a resampled-every-step delay isn't a small change (the agent would need to reason about a delay it can't observe in advance) …

augmentation.md §5 — “Known limitation: exact for fixed delay, approximate for stochastic/time-varying”

That last clause describes an inference problem, written down before the experiment that failed to test it. conf/delay/stochastic.yaml and conf/delay/time_varying.yaml have existed since the start of the project and have never been run: all 102 runs used delay=none or delay=fixed value=1.

Three confounds, restated as design faults

C-1 · capacity

Capacity and architecture are perfectly confounded

Every recursive trunk holds ≈788k parameters; every MLP holds ≈135k. No cell varies one while holding the other. Since the 797k Transformer reproduces the value-fit deficit and the 167k LSTM does not, capacity is currently the better-supported explanation — running opposite to the direction the design assumed.

C-2 · memory

The port implements the weaker latent variant

TRMTrunk.forward expands H_init/L_init fresh on every call, so nothing persists between environment steps. HRM-Agent compares exactly this choice and finds carrying the latent converges faster in 4 of 5 runs. A trunk with no cross-step state cannot estimate state.

C-3 · the task

The delay condition leaves nothing to infer

Fixed, known, one-step delay with augmentation is a solved estimation problem. The recursion had no residual uncertainty to resolve, so it contributed capacity and cost and nothing else — which is precisely the measured outcome.

A fourth gap, for completeness

project-config.yaml names the delay axis as “observation delay, actuation delay, and jitter”. DelayedEnv implements actuation delay only — it queues the agent's action and applies an older one; the observation returned is always current. Observation delay and jitter have no implementation and no config group. They sit in the exploration inventory (§07, items B7–B8) rather than on the critical path, but the project should stop describing them as tested axes.

The consequence for the current report is specific: its programme item E1 (a capacity-matched MLP) is only half the fix, because growing the MLP leaves the comparison at one capacity level; and E2 (Carry-Z) is not the “contained change” it is described as, because off-policy replay samples transitions with no latent attached. Both are re-specified below.

02

The Phase 3 hypothesis

H3 · residual-uncertainty hypothesis

Weight-tied recursive computation earns its cost in model-free continuous control only where two conditions hold jointly: (i) the conditioning leaves residual state uncertainty that iteration could resolve, and (ii) the trunk can carry information across decision steps, so the iteration has something to accumulate.

Phases 1–2 satisfied neither. Their null is the outcome H3 predicts. Phase 3's job is to build the conditions under which H3 makes a different prediction from the alternatives — then check.

The alternatives are not “recursion works”. They are the two deflationary accounts the data currently supports: Hcap, that the deficits track parameter count under a fixed sample budget and recursion is irrelevant either way; and Hnull, that recursive refinement belongs to supervised, revisitable, compositionally-correctable problems and does not transfer here under any conditioning.

H3.2 is the load-bearing row, and it is deliberately an interaction rather than a main effect. A capacity explanation cannot produce a sign flip across delay conditions using identical networks, and neither can a tuning artefact that applies uniformly. A predicted change of sign is the strongest thing this design can ask for at six seeds.

Why this is consistent with the data, not a rescue of it

H3 requires no Phase 1/2 number to be wrong. It reinterprets them: 0/6 seed-pairs at fixed-known delay is what H3 predicts; a value-fit deficit shared with the Transformer is what H3 predicts; no difference between trm, trm_hier6 and trm_singlez is what H3 predicts, since varying the recursive structure changes nothing when the recursion has no signal to work on.

And H3 can be held to a prediction: if the interaction does not appear, H3 is dead, and the conclusion becomes Hnull with all three confounds removed — a considerably stronger negative than the one currently drafted, and one that closes the question instead of leaving it open.

03

The missing axis: is any of this a usable controller?

The project scores trunks on return, training wall-clock and parameter count. A controls or robotics reader needs three things none of those supply — and all three are cheap.

Command quality

A controller that achieves the same return while commanding twice the actuator rate is a worse controller. Actuator wear, thermal load, gearbox backlash and vibration scale with command rate, not with reward. Control effort ∫u²dt, command rate |Δu|, jerk |Δ²u|, saturation fraction, sign-reversal rate and the fraction of command power above a cutoff are all computable directly from rollouts.

Robustness envelope, not point performance

Every number in the project so far was measured under exactly the conditions the policy trained in. A controller is judged on margin: how far the plant can drift, how much the delay can vary, how noisy the sensor can get before performance collapses. Since delay-robustness is this project's founding axis, the absence of any off-nominal measurement is the single largest gap in the evidence base.

Real-time feasibility

Training wall-clock is the wrong cost for deployment; per-decision inference latency against the control period is the right one. At H_cycles=3, L_cycles=6 the TRM trunk performs 21 applications of the shared cell per forward pass — 18 inner updates plus 3 outer — each two SwiGLU blocks, against the MLP's two matrix multiplies. Whether that lands at 0.4 ms or 4 ms at batch 1 on a CPU decides whether the trunk is admissible at 100 Hz, at 500 Hz, or not at all. The project has never measured it. It is a one-hour experiment.

Does the finding generalise?

entry: G2 shows an interaction

S3.1 · Algorithm cross-check — 24 jobs

DDPG is documented as brittle on two of our three environments, and its OU exploration is itself a confound on the command-regularity measure. src/algos/td3.py is implemented and has never been used by this project. Re-run the decisive contrast — 2 trunks × {C1, C3} × 6 seeds — under arch=td3. If the interaction reproduces, the finding is not DDPG-specific; if it inverts, that is a finding in itself.

S3.2 · A rung chosen for control-theoretic reasons — 9 pilot + 48

Replace BipedalWalker-v3 with InvertedDoublePendulum-v5. The rationale is specific rather than “harder”: it is an open-loop unstable plant with a short time constant, so delay directly consumes stability margin — delay does not merely obscure the state, it destabilises. It gives natural control metrics (time-to-fall, RMS pole angle, control effort), trains fast, and MuJoCo has been Apache-2.0 and pip-installable for years, so the licence gating recorded in earlier phases is stale and should be rechecked rather than inherited.

Pilot first: 1 trunk × 3 seeds × budget ∈ {200, 600, 1500} episodes, to find the budget at which the baseline reaches the solved threshold. Phase 2's floor effect was caused precisely by skipping this step; no full matrix is submitted until a pilot shows the environment is learnable within budget. Then 4 trunks × {C1, C3} × 6 seeds = 48.

S3.3 · Retire the floor-effect environments

MountainCarContinuous-v0 and BipedalWalker-v3 stay in the report as documented failure modes but are not re-run at higher budget: a third environment adds nothing until the mechanism question is settled on a rung that works.

S4

Restore the training discipline

entry: G3 passed — refinement continuity has traction

Three variants of the recursive trunk at fixed parameters, {C1, C3}, 6 seeds:

  • (a) No truncation. Backpropagate through all outer cycles. Currently 14 of 21 cell applications receive no gradient at all.
  • (b) Per-cycle supervision. Apply the critic loss to every outer cycle's readout — a deep-supervision surrogate for the mechanism ARC Prize identify as HRM's true driver, and the closest available analogue to TRM's supervision over halt_max_steps.
  • (c) Fixed-point / DEQ readout. Iterate to convergence and differentiate implicitly, removing truncation entirely at constant memory. The most interesting and the most speculative.

This is the only stage that can produce a constructive positive result — the mechanism transfers when its training discipline is ported with it — rather than a conditional one.

What would make Phase 3 a success

Not a positive result. 05

A signal already sitting in the banked data

Running the command-quality measure over the existing step_history — 102 runs, no new compute — turns up a pattern the project has never reported. Every non-MLP trunk commands a markedly smoother action sequence than the MLP.

Three reasons this is a candidate signal and not yet a finding

All three are what Stage 0.2 is built to settle, and they are stated here rather than dressed over.

  1. The actions are exploration-noisy. DDPG adds OU noise inside _select_action, so the statistic mixes policy behaviour with a common noise process. Stage 0.2 measures deterministic rollouts.
  2. A dead policy is maximally smooth. The LSTM scores smoothest on Pendulum while returning −1460 — untrained. Regularity is interpretable only jointly with return, which is why Stage 0.2 reports a Pareto front rather than a ranking.
  3. It is not recursion-specific either. The LSTM and Transformer are smoother than TRM. The honest reading is that the plain MLP is the outlier in command regularity, and the question worth asking is whether that costs it anything in a real actuator — not whether TRM is special. Same confound shape as the report's §7.3, and reported the same way.

Carry-Z is the plan's main technical risk. Carrying a latent across environment steps is easy at acting time and wrong at update time: off-policy replay samples i.i.d. transitions with no latent attached, so the update would refine from a cold latent while the behaviour policy refined from a warm one. Three options, in descending preference — stored state (R2D2-style: write the carry into the buffer and use the stored, slightly stale value during updates; cheapest, well-precedented, introduces drift as weights move); burn-in (store short sequences and recompute the carry over the prefix under no_grad; more faithful, roughly k× the update cost, needs a sequence buffer); and carry at act, reset at update, which is rejected outright because it makes the acting and learning policies structurally different and would confound exactly the comparison Stage 2 exists to make.

Whichever is chosen gets recorded in the report as a design decision on the same footing as Reset-Z was. That is how C-2 happened in the first place.
